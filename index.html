Once a process gets the CPU it keeps it until its whole burst is done. The scheduler only decides when the CPU becomes free. A process that arrives in the meantime waits, even if it is much shorter.

Preemptive: the CPU can be taken away mid-run

The scheduler may stop the running process before it finishes and give the CPU to another one. The stopped process goes back to waiting and later resumes where it left off; no work is lost or repeated.

Under SRTF, B needs 2 while A still needs 5, so B takes the CPU at 1. A resumes at 3, so it appears in two pieces. RR does the same when the quantum runs out, whatever the lengths.

Preemptive here: SRTF (a strictly shorter arrival) · RR (the quantum timer)

Quantum: the longest single turn in Round Robin

In Round Robin each process may use the CPU for at most one quantum at a time. When its quantum runs out and it is not finished, it goes to the back of the queue and the next process gets a turn. A process that finishes early simply gives up the rest of its turn. Only RR uses the quantum, so the field appears only when RR is selected.

They alternate in turns of 2. B needs only 1 unit in its second turn, so it ends at 7 and A finishes at 8. A smaller quantum gives everyone an earlier first turn but chops the work into more pieces (switches are free here; a real OS pays for each one). A quantum at least as long as every burst means nobody is ever cut off, and RR behaves like FCFS.

    How each policy chooses the next process

    Non-preemptive

    The CPU is never taken away. Once a process starts, it runs until its whole burst is done. Others wait, even if a shorter one arrives. On the chart every process is one unbroken block.

    FCFS · SJF

    Preemptive

    The CPU can be taken away mid-run. The interrupted process goes back to waiting and later resumes where it stopped, so it can appear in several pieces. SRTF interrupts when a strictly shorter process arrives; RR interrupts when the quantum runs out.

    SRTF · RR

    1. FCFS · First-Come, First-Served Non-preemptive

      The process that arrived first runs until it finishes. Then the next earliest arrival runs.

    2. SJF · Shortest Job First Non-preemptive

      Whenever the CPU becomes free, the waiting process with the shortest burst runs until it finishes. A running process is never interrupted.

    3. SRTF · Shortest Remaining Time First Preemptive

      The process with the least work left runs. A new arrival interrupts it only if it needs strictly less time; on a tie, the running process continues.

    4. RR · Round Robin Preemptive

      Processes take turns of at most one quantum in a first-in, first-out queue. A process that arrives exactly when a turn ends joins the queue before the process that was just interrupted. The Edge case workload shows this.
